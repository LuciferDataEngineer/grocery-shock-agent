# Where the grocery basket hurts — September 2026

These findings are read from the gold tables built on the committed ABS and RBA snapshots. Access date: 6 November 2026.

## The decision in one page

Overall prices were **up 4.3%** in September 2026. Food and drinks were **up 3.2%** and added **0.56 points** to that national rise. The food group is not the main driver.

Housing was up 5.2% and added 1.14 points. Transport was up 8.7% and added 0.99 points. Petrol alone was up 23.6% and added 0.77 points. Electricity was up 12.8%.

The cash rate target is **4.85%**. It was raised 5 times in 2026. Mortgage repayments are not inside the grocery price index, so households can be squeezed by the rate and by a few shelf prices at the same time.

## What households should do

- Treat fruit (up 10.2%, after 1.8% in August) as a spike, not the new normal for the whole shop. Vegetables were up 2.1%.
- Beef is still up 5.8%, down from 12.6% in July. Pork was down 1.3%, poultry up 0.4%, fish up 1.7%. Eggs were down 2.3%.
- Breakfast cereals (up 8.5%) and milk (up 5.4%) are the other hot supermarket lines. Cheese was up 1.8% and bread up 2.3%.
- Eating out adds more to the national rise (0.28 points) than fruit (0.09 points) because it has a much larger weight.

Price gaps are not diet advice. They only say which published lines moved less.

## What policymakers should watch next

1. Petrol and transport. The September yearly rise in petrol is large enough to move the national figure on its own.
2. Housing, including electricity (up 12.8%) and rents (up 3.6%).
3. Whether the fruit spike fades next month. It jumped from 1.8% in August to 10.2% in September.
4. Whether beef keeps cooling. The shelf price is still high, but the yearly pace has already fallen since July.
5. The cash rate at 4.85% after this year's rises. It will not show up inside the food index, and it still changes household budgets.

## What not to conclude

- Do not treat the food group (up 3.2%) as the hottest part of the national basket. It is rising more slowly than overall prices.
- Do not read housing prices in this index as mortgage interest. The cash rate is a separate series from the Reserve Bank.
- A big shelf jump on a small line (fruit, breakfast cereals) hurts that shopper and barely moves the national index. A moderate rise on petrol or housing does the opposite.
